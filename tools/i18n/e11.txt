2621	The priority matrix (Eisenhower matrix) sorts tasks by "important" and "urgent" into four groups:
2622	① Important & urgent — do now; ② Important, not urgent — schedule it; this is where your time is best spent; ③ Urgent, not important — handle quickly or ask for help; ④ Not important, not urgent — do less or skip.
2623	How to use: write down everything, then place each in a box. Clear box ① first each day, then save big blocks of time for box ②.
2624	The PDCA cycle (Deming cycle) splits a task into four steps and improves it round by round:
2625	P Plan: set goals and methods; D Do: carry out the plan; C Check: compare results with goals and find problems; A Act: keep what works and carry unsolved problems into the next plan.
2626	Good for: exam prep, learning a skill, preparing for competitions — anything that needs repeated improvement.
2627	The SMART principle turns vague wishes into actionable goals. A good goal meets five criteria:
2628	S Specific: say exactly what you'll achieve; M Measurable: use numbers to tell when it's done; A Achievable: within reach with effort; R Relevant: related to what you really want; T Time-bound: has a clear deadline.
2629	Example: change "I want to get good at English" into "Memorize 30 CET-4 words every day before Nov 30 and score 80% on weekend self-tests".
2630	The six-task method (Ivy Lee method):
2631	1. Before bed, write down the 6 most important things for tomorrow; 2. Order them by importance; 3. Next day, start with #1 and finish it before moving on; 4. Move anything unfinished to the next day's list.
2632	It forces you to prioritize and focus on one thing at a time, so a long to-do list doesn't scare you.
2633	Pomodoro technique: pick one task and focus on it for 25 minutes (one "Pomodoro") without checking your phone; then rest 5 minutes; after every 4 Pomodoros, rest 15–30 minutes.
2634	Tips: break big tasks into Pomodoro-sized chunks; if interrupted, write it down and deal with it after the Pomodoro. The timer below is ready to use.
